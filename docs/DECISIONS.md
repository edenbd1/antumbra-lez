# Design decision log: Antumbra Vesting

This log records the major design decisions behind `antumbra_vesting`, the
vesting program in this repository, and behind the arithmetic, tooling and
evidence that support it. Each entry gives the context, the options that were
on the table, the option chosen, the rationale, the trade-offs, and where the
decision is implemented or proven.

Dates are the dates of the commits that made the decision, from `git log`.
An entry is **accepted** while it describes the current program on LEZ v0.3,
and **superseded** when a later entry replaced it; superseded entries are kept,
because the v0.2.4 deployment and its evidence are still in the repository
and read against them. The log is updated when a decision is made or changed,
and is current at each milestone delivery.

Current program: LEZ `v0.3.0` (`db66590a`), header
`FCrja8g2ZKvxZwNZchdppKWQCDxPNUHxnEMidrmqrt6X`, ImageID `72d5cdc0…71a425f4`
(see [`DEPLOYMENTS.md`](../DEPLOYMENTS.md)). The port itself is described in
[`docs/v03-port-plan.md`](v03-port-plan.md).

## D-01: Integer-only arithmetic, rounding toward solvency

- **Date:** 2026-08-20 (`b5bcba1`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** A zkVM guest has no floating point worth trusting, and every unit
an escrow pays out must exist in it. Linear vesting divides `total × elapsed`
by a duration, which is rarely exact. A cancellation splits the total three
ways: already claimed, vested but unclaimed, and unvested.

**Options considered.**
1. Fixed-point or floating arithmetic with rounding to nearest.
2. Integer arithmetic rounding down at every step, with the end of the
   schedule reached through the general formula.
3. Integer arithmetic rounding down, the end of the schedule returning `total`
   directly, and the three cancellation parts derived from one `vested_at` call.

**Chosen.** Option 3.

**Rationale.** Rounding down keeps the residue in escrow, so it is paid on a
later claim rather than early, and no prefix of claims can exceed what has
vested. Returning `total` at the end makes claims over a fully elapsed schedule
sum to the total exactly; routing the end through the division would make
exactness depend on the division being exact, which it is not in general.
Deriving claimed, vested-but-unclaimed and unvested from one `vested_at` call
means the three cannot drift apart and always sum to the total.

**Trade-offs.** A beneficiary may receive up to one unit less at an intermediate
claim than a real-valued formula gives; it is paid later. The end of the
schedule is a special case in the code, and has to be tested and proven as one.

**Where.** [`src/vesting.rs`](../src/vesting.rs) (module header, `cancel` at
[src/vesting.rs:257](../src/vesting.rs#L257), `linear_between` at
[src/vesting.rs:296](../src/vesting.rs#L296)); on chain, `unvested` and the
exact-refund check in
[`programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs`](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs)
(refund check at
[programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs:894](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs#L894)).
Proven by the Kani harnesses `everything_vests_by_the_end` and
`cancel_splits_the_total_exactly` (D-27), and on testnet by the manifest checks
"refund is exactly the unvested part" and "token refund plus escrow plus
claimed is the total" in [`evidence/v03/testnet.tsv`](../evidence/v03/testnet.tsv).

## D-02: No cached vested amount: `vested_at` is a pure function

- **Date:** 2026-08-20 (`b5bcba1`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** A vesting position changes value with time. A program can either
store a running "vested" figure and update it, or recompute it on demand.

**Options considered.**
1. Store a vested amount and refresh it on each interaction or on a periodic
   poke.
2. Store only the terms, `claimed` and the cancellation instant, and compute the
   vested amount from them at a given time on every call.

**Chosen.** Option 2.

**Rationale.** With no cached figure there is no window in which a claim prices
against a stale value, and no refresh step to forget. The computation has no
input-dependent branch beyond the schedule phase, so its cost is flat across
schedule shape and elapsed time (`5a82c9f`), which matters against a per
transaction execution cap.

**Trade-offs.** Each claim and cancellation recomputes the schedule; the cost is
measured and small (see [`executor-tests/CYCLES.md`](../executor-tests/CYCLES.md)).
The v0.3 port kept this unchanged: the program rebuilds
`antumbra::vesting::Schedule` from stored terms on every call.

**Where.** `vested_at` at [src/vesting.rs:173](../src/vesting.rs#L173);
`vested`, `claimable` and `unvested` in
[`programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs`](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs);
"What does not change" in [`docs/v03-port-plan.md`](v03-port-plan.md).

## D-03: Milestones as a bitmap, equal tranches on chain

- **Date:** 2026-08-20 (`b5bcba1`, `5a82c9f`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** Milestone vesting unlocks tranches when an authority signals them.
Signalling must be idempotent: a second signal of the same milestone must not
release anything.

**Options considered.**
1. A list of signalled milestones, searched before each signal.
2. A fixed-width bitmap, signalling as a compare-and-set on one bit.

**Chosen.** Option 2, with at most 64 tranches in a `u64`. On chain the
tranches are equal: the released amount is `⌊total × signalled / tranches⌋`.

**Rationale.** Idempotence becomes a property of the data structure rather than
a check someone has to remember. When every bit is set the numerator equals the
denominator, so the holding reaches exactly zero with no residue (`04e3a54`).

**Trade-offs.** At most 64 milestones per schedule, and on chain the tranches
are equal rather than individually sized.

**Where.** `MAX_TRANCHES` and `milestone_vested` in
[`programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs`](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs);
Kani harnesses `milestone_signal_is_idempotent` and
`equal_tranche_amount_is_bounded_and_complete` in
[`src/vesting/proofs.rs`](../src/vesting/proofs.rs); on testnet, the manifest
rows `signal-0`, `signal-0-again` (refused) and `claim-milestones`.

## D-04: One claim instruction that pays and records together

- **Date:** 2026-08-21 (`04e3a54`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** Before payouts worked on v0.2.4, the program had a
`record_claim` instruction that advanced a schedule's `claimed` without paying.

**Options considered.**
1. Keep `record_claim` beside the paying claim.
2. Delete it, so the only way to advance `claimed` is the instruction that pays.

**Chosen.** Option 2.

**Rationale.** Two instructions where one records a claim that never paid is the
state a vesting program exists to prevent. On v0.3 the same property holds
structurally: the payout is a chained call of the same transaction, and a panic
in `apply` rolls back the whole transaction, payout included.

**Trade-offs.** None identified beyond losing a debugging aid.

**Where.** `Instruction::Claim` and `Effect::Claim` in
[`programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs`](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs).

## D-05: Time as an instruction argument

- **Date:** 2026-08-20 (`8c09b33`)
- **Status:** superseded by D-06 (2026-09-26)

**Context.** A zkVM guest has no clock. The first v0.2.4 program took `now` as an
argument to `claim` and `cancel`.

**Options considered.** Trust a caller-supplied time; read a chain-written clock.

**Chosen at the time.** The caller's time, refusing only a time earlier than the
schedule's `last_seen`.

**Why it was replaced.** A beneficiary could claim "at the end date" on the first
day, and a creator could cancel "at the start" and take back what had vested
(`88cf776`).

**Where.** The program at that commit:
[antumbra_vesting.rs at `8c09b33`](https://github.com/edenbd1/antumbra-lez/blob/8c09b33ba2b3ea8059ad59f6f0e22f2f398ff2e7/programs/vesting/src/bin/antumbra_vesting.rs).

## D-06: Time from the LEZ clock accounts (v0.2.4)

- **Date:** 2026-09-26 (`88cf776`)
- **Status:** superseded by D-08 (2026-10-01)

**Context.** v0.2.4 exposes sequencer-written clock accounts (per block, every
10 blocks, every 50 blocks), owned by the clock program.

**Options considered.**
1. Accept any account that claims to be a clock.
2. Check the owner only, or the address only.
3. Check both the owner (the clock program's pinned ImageID) and the exact
   address.

**Chosen at the time.** Option 3; `cancel` required the per-block clock.

**Rationale.** Either check alone is satisfied by an account the caller wrote.
Times are milliseconds, the clock's unit, and each claim and cancellation
recorded the reading it used.

**Why it was replaced.** A v0.3 plan cannot read the clock, or any account data
(D-08).

**Where.** `now()` in the v0.2.4 program:
[antumbra_vesting.rs at `6cd55c3`](https://github.com/edenbd1/antumbra-lez/blob/6cd55c393814e1715de426c57e8ce43320681dc5/programs/vesting/src/bin/antumbra_vesting.rs);
the v0.2.4 evidence page [`evidence/VESTING.md`](../evidence/VESTING.md).

## D-07: Private claims read the 50-block clock (v0.2.4)

- **Date:** 2026-09-26 (`9aeedf7`)
- **Status:** superseded by D-08 and D-16 (2026-10-01)

**Context.** On v0.2.4 a privacy-preserving transaction is proved against its
public inputs as they were when proving began, and re-checked at inclusion. The
per-block clock changes every block and a proof takes minutes, so every private
claim that read it was silently dropped.

**Options considered.**
1. Leave private claims unsupported.
2. Let `claim` also accept the 10- and 50-block clocks, with the same owner and
   exact-address checks; keep `cancel` on the per-block clock.

**Chosen at the time.** Option 2.

**Rationale.** A coarser clock reads earlier than the chain, which can only make
a claim pay less than has vested, never more; the rest stays claimable. An
earlier reading on `cancel` would let the authority take back what had vested,
so `cancel` did not get it.

**Why it was replaced.** On v0.3 the public effects of a private transaction are
applied at settlement and the claim's window is `[t, ∞)`, so there is no coarse
clock and no drift refusal (port plan, "Private claim" row).

**Where.** [`docs/v03-port-plan.md`](v03-port-plan.md);
[`evidence/VESTING.md`](../evidence/VESTING.md).

## D-08: Time from transaction validity windows (v0.3)

- **Date:** 2026-10-01 (`6cd55c3`, `c08d637`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** In the v0.3 model a plan sees account ids and signer flags, never
data, so it cannot read a clock account. It can set a timestamp validity window
that the sequencer checks against the block time.

**Options considered.**
1. Trust a caller-supplied time (rejected already in D-05).
2. Have the claim and the cancellation name a time `t` and bound the
   transaction's validity around it.

**Chosen.** Option 2: a claim at `t` is valid from `t` onward (`[t, ∞)`); a
cancellation at `t` is valid only in `[t, t + 120 s)` (`CANCEL_WINDOW_MS =
120000`). `apply` also refuses a cancellation that names a time before the
schedule's last claim (`last_seen`).

**Rationale.** A caller can only claim against a time that has already happened,
and a claim dated later than the chain's clock is refused by the sequencer. An
authority cannot back-date a cancellation by more than two minutes, so cannot
freeze accrual further in the past than that, and cannot cancel before an instant at
which a claim was already paid, which would refund that part twice.

**Trade-offs.** A claim names a time slightly behind the chain and so may pay a
little less than is vested at inclusion; the rest stays claimable. A
cancellation must land within two minutes of the time it names. The schedule
records `last_seen`, the largest time any claim or the cancellation named.

**Where.** `CANCEL_WINDOW_MS` at
[programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs:54](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs#L54),
the claim window at
[programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs:684](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs#L684)
and the cancel window at
[programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs:723](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs#L723);
executor tests `a_cancellation_cannot_be_back_dated_past_its_window` and
`a_cancellation_cannot_name_a_time_before_the_last_claim` in
[`executor-tests/tests/program.rs`](../executor-tests/tests/program.rs); on
testnet, manifest row `claim-in-the-future` (refused).

## D-09: A claim carries (amount, t); apply checks it against the schedule

- **Date:** 2026-10-01 (`c08d637`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** In v0.3 the payout is a chained call decided in `plan`, which cannot
read the stored schedule. The amount owed is only known in `apply`, which sees
the schedule shard but cannot emit calls.

**Options considered.**
1. Pay "everything claimable", as the v0.2.4 program did, which the plan cannot
   compute.
2. The caller names `amount` and `t`; the plan pays exactly `amount` out of the
   escrow; `apply` refuses unless `amount ≤ vested(t) − claimed`.

**Chosen.** Option 2.

**Rationale.** A refusal in `apply` panics and rolls back the whole transaction,
payout included, so an over-claim moves nothing. The CLI computes the exact
claimable amount locally and refuses to send more unless forced, which is how
refusals were put on chain on purpose (D-30).

**Trade-offs.** Clients must compute `amount` and `t` themselves; a stale
computation produces a refused transaction rather than a smaller payout.

**Where.** `Effect::Claim` in `apply`, at
[programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs:851](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs#L851);
core test `a_claim_is_bounded_by_what_has_vested_at_the_time_it_names` in
[`programs/vesting/core/src/tests.rs`](../programs/vesting/core/src/tests.rs);
the pre-check in [`cli/src/main.rs`](../cli/src/main.rs); on testnet, manifest
rows `claim-overclaim` (refused, escrow untouched) and `claim-linear`.

## D-10: Signer checks move to apply in the plan/apply model

- **Date:** 2026-10-01 (`c08d637`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** On v0.2.4 one call read the whole schedule and the signers together,
and SPEL's `#[account(signer)]` constraint checked the signature.
On v0.3 the plan sees which rows are signed but not who the schedule names as
beneficiary, creator or authority.

**Options considered.**
1. Trust any signer in the plan.
2. The plan requires the signer row to carry a signature, and passes the
   signer's id in the effect; `apply` compares it with the stored field.

**Chosen.** Option 2. The signer appears as a "signer handle": a row naming the
signing account with this program's own shard selected, which the program never
writes, so the same account can also appear with its native or token shard as a
destination.

**Rationale.** It is the only place both facts are visible: signature in the
plan, stored role in `apply`. A mismatch panics and rolls back the transaction.

**Trade-offs.** Every instruction carries an extra row; clients build rows with
the shared `rows` module (D-21) so the order cannot disagree.

**Where.** `expect_signer_handle` and the `apply` checks in
[`programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs`](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs);
executor tests `only_the_beneficiary_claims` and `an_unsigned_claim_is_refused`;
on testnet, manifest rows `claim-by-creator`, `cancel-by-beneficiary`,
`signal-by-creator`, `transfer-by-creator`, `claim-by-old-holder`,
`nc-by-beneficiary` and `cancel-by-creator-not-authority`, all refused.

## D-11: Creation refuses to overwrite a schedule

- **Date:** 2026-08-20 (`8c09b33`); mechanism moved on 2026-10-01 (`c08d637`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** A schedule lives at the PDA of its id. A second creation at the same
id would overwrite terms and, on v0.3, fund the escrow a second time.

**Options considered.** Allow re-creation; refuse only different bytes; refuse
any existing bytes.

**Chosen.** Refuse any existing schedule. On v0.2.4 this was SPEL's `init`
constraint. On v0.3 nothing is refused unless a program refuses it, so the
`Create` effect's `apply` asserts that the schedule shard is empty.

**Rationale.** Refusing any existing bytes, rather than only different ones,
keeps a replayed creation from landing and paying into the escrow again.

**Trade-offs.** A schedule id is single-use; clients must choose fresh ids.

**Where.** [programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs:833](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs#L833);
executor test `a_duplicate_creation_is_refused_and_takes_no_second_funding`;
on testnet, manifest row `create-duplicate` (refused) and the check "duplicate
took no second funding".

## D-12: Escrow created by `create`, filled by separate `fund_*` instructions (v0.2.4)

- **Date:** 2026-08-21 (`1413eba`)
- **Status:** superseded by D-13 (2026-10-01)

**Context.** On v0.2.4 an account could not be initialised and paid into in the
same transaction: the chained transfer read a pre-state the initialisation had
not written.

**Chosen at the time.** `create_schedule` created the holding and
`fund_schedule`, `fund_token_schedule` and `fund_batch` filled it.

**Trade-offs at the time.** A created schedule could exist unfunded between the
two transactions, and the evidence had to mark a check that passed on a
never-funded escrow as vacuous (`225bdab`).

**Why it was replaced.** v0.3 lets the creation transaction carry the transfer
(D-13).

**Where.** The v0.2.4 IDL, [`idl/antumbra_vesting.idl.json`](../idl/antumbra_vesting.idl.json).

## D-13: Escrow funded in the creation transaction (v0.3)

- **Date:** 2026-10-01 (`c08d637`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** On v0.3 a plan can write the schedule effect and chain a transfer
into the escrow in the same transaction.

**Options considered.** Keep the separate `fund_*` instructions; fund in
`create`.

**Chosen.** `create_schedule` and `create_schedule_batch` transfer exactly
`terms.total` (or `terms.total × n`) from the creator into the escrow, in the
same transaction. The `fund_*` instructions were removed.

**Rationale.** A schedule can no longer exist without its escrow, and the amount
is fixed by the terms rather than by a second call. If the creator cannot pay,
the whole creation is refused and nothing is written.

**Trade-offs.** The creator must hold the full amount at creation time.

**Where.** `Instruction::CreateSchedule` in
[`programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs`](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs);
executor tests `a_creation_the_creator_cannot_fund_writes_nothing` and
`a_linear_native_schedule_pays_exactly_what_has_vested`; on testnet, the check
"linear escrow funded exactly = 600" after `create-linear`.

## D-14: Escrow custody on v0.2.4: program-owned native PDA, seeded token transfer

- **Date:** 2026-08-21 (`bc2f4b0`, `1413eba`, `b5aa3da`); token escrow 2026-09-26 (`88cf776`)
- **Status:** superseded by D-15 (2026-10-01)

**Context.** The first payout design chained a transfer out of the escrow, and
the runtime refused it: a chained transfer debits an account only if it is
authorised, and the escrow does not sign a claim. Requiring it to would put the
creator in the loop for every claim.

**Chosen at the time.** For native balance, make the escrow a PDA owned by the
vesting program, which debits its own account and credits the beneficiary
directly, with no chained call. For tokens, a token-program holding paid out by
a chained `Transfer` carrying this program's PDA seed. Callee programs were
pinned by ImageID, after the buy path of a sibling program was found chainable
into an arbitrary program (`b5aa3da`).

**Why it was replaced.** v0.3 has no program-owned accounts; balances are shards
of any account (D-15).

**Where.** [`evidence/VESTING.md`](../evidence/VESTING.md); historical section
of [`DEPLOYMENTS.md`](../DEPLOYMENTS.md).

## D-15: Escrow custody on v0.3: `custody_transfer` and seeded token transfer from a header PDA

- **Date:** 2026-10-01 (`c08d637`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** On v0.3 an account carries a map of shards keyed by program id;
native balance is the shard at `[0; 32]`. A program is addressed by its header
account, and PDAs derive from the header id.

**Options considered.** Hold escrow in a key-controlled account; hold it in a
PDA of this program and pay out by chained calls that carry the PDA seed.

**Chosen.** The escrow is the PDA `[schedule_id, "holding"]` of the program
header (`[batch_id, "holding"]` for a batch). Native payouts use
`native_token::custody_transfer` with the holding's seed; token payouts use the
token program's `Transfer` with the seed attached. The token program's id is
derived from its builtin name, not read from anything the caller passes.

**Rationale.** Nobody holds a key for the escrow, and only this program can
attach its seed, so only this program can debit it. Deriving the token program
id means a payout cannot be routed through a lookalike program. Multi-seed PDAs
keep SPEL's rule (one seed direct, several hashed with SHA-256) so addresses read
the same way as on v0.2.4. Because PDAs derive from the header, an upgrade of
the header keeps every schedule and escrow at its address (runbook, "Upgrades").

**Trade-offs.** All v0.2.4 addresses changed with the port. The escrow row in an
instruction must select the asset's shard, which the plan checks.

**Where.** `payout`, `holding_seed` and `token_program` in
[`programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs`](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs)
(payout at
[programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs:580-592](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs#L580-L592));
executor test `a_token_schedule_escrows_claims_and_refunds_the_token`; on
testnet, manifest rows `create-token`, `claim-token`, `cancel-token`.

## D-16: Private claims through privacy-preserving transactions

- **Date:** 2026-09-26 (`88cf776`); v0.3 path 2026-10-01 (`c08d637`, `457227a`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** A beneficiary should be able to receive vested funds without the
amount being public.

**Options considered.**
1. Pay only into the signing key, publicly.
2. Pay into a destination the beneficiary names, public or shielded, and let a
   claim be sent as a privacy-preserving transaction.

**Chosen.** Option 2. The claim's destination row is chosen by the beneficiary;
the CLI's `claim --to Private/<id>` proves the transaction locally against the
committed guest. On v0.3 the public effects of a private transaction are
applied at settlement, so the schedule records the claim publicly while the
amount lands in a shielded account visible only to its owner's wallet.

**Rationale.** The program only ever increases the destination, and any program
may, so naming a shielded destination needs no extra authority. Private
transactions are fee-exempt on v0.3.

**Trade-offs.** A private claim takes minutes to prove on the client. It emits no
event (D-22). The schedule's `claimed` field is still public.

**Where.** [`cli/src/main.rs`](../cli/src/main.rs) (private claim path);
[`scripts/e2e-v03.sh`](../scripts/e2e-v03.sh); on testnet, manifest rows
`claim-private-native` and `claim-private-token`, each followed by the checks
"recorded on the schedule" and "the wallet decrypted the claim into the shielded
account".

## D-17: Refund account fixed at creation, nominated authorities

- **Date:** 2026-09-26 (`88cf776`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** Cancellation and milestone signalling need an authority, and the
unvested part of a cancellation must go somewhere.

**Options considered.** Let whoever cancels name the refund destination; fix it
at creation. Make the creator the only authority; allow nominated authorities.

**Chosen.** A `refund_to` account fixed at creation, which `apply` requires a
cancellation to match, and a nominated cancel authority and milestone authority,
each defaulting to the creator.

**Rationale.** A cancel authority cannot redirect the unvested part. Nominated
authorities let a creator delegate without sharing keys. Positions can be made
non-cancelable (one-way) and are transferable only if created so, and only by
the holder.

**Trade-offs.** The refund account cannot be changed after creation. The v0.2.4
program also refused a refund account equal to the cancel authority; the v0.3
code requires only a non-zero refund account.

**Where.** `new_state` and `Effect::Cancel` in
[`programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs`](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs);
executor tests `a_nominated_cancel_authority_cancels_and_the_creator_cannot`
and `non_cancelable_is_one_way_and_the_creators_alone`; on testnet, manifest
rows `cancel-wrong-refund` (refused), `cancel-by-authority`,
`make-non-cancelable`, `cancel-after-nc` (refused).

## D-18: Batch creation over one shared holding

- **Date:** 2026-09-27 (`ca8dc1b`); kept on v0.3 2026-10-01 (`c08d637`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** Creators need schedules for many recipients in one operation. On
v0.2.4 a transaction in which two chained calls debit the same payer was
refused, so one transaction could not fund N separate escrows.

**Options considered.** N separate creation transactions; one transaction with N
escrows; one transaction with N schedules over one shared holding filled by one
transfer.

**Chosen.** The shared holding. Schedule `i` of batch `B` has id
`SHA-256(B ‖ i)` and lives at the PDA of that id, checked before anything is
written, so a batch schedule is an ordinary schedule to every read path. Each
schedule keeps its own total, claimed amount and cancellation, and every claim
or cancel debits only what that schedule is owed. On v0.3 the creator funds the
holding with `total × n` in the creation transaction (D-13).

**Trade-offs.** All schedules of a batch share terms and one escrow account; the
correctness of each share rests on the per-schedule accounting rather than on
separate balances.

**Where.** `Instruction::CreateScheduleBatch` and `batch_schedule_id` in
[`programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs`](../programs/vesting/core/src/lib.rs);
executor tests `a_batch_is_funded_once_and_each_schedule_moves_only_its_share`
and `a_batch_row_out_of_order_is_refused`; on testnet, manifest rows `batch-8`,
`batch-claim-0`, `batch-cancel-1` and the check "batch holding lost only
schedule 0's claim and schedule 1's refund".

## D-22: One event per instruction, none for a private claim

## D-27: Model-check the arithmetic with Kani

- **Date:** 2026-09-28 (`0c457af`)
- **Status:** accepted

**Context.** Tests try some inputs. The vesting invariants (D-01, D-03) should
hold for every input.

**Options considered.** More tests and property tests; model checking with Kani.

**Chosen.** Eleven Kani harnesses over every `u64` timestamp, with totals and
durations bounded to 8 bits so the solver closes each one: nothing vests past
the total, everything vests by the end, nothing before the cliff, a claim pays
exactly the vested unpaid amount, cancellation splits exactly and freezes
vesting, a milestone cannot be signalled twice, the equal-tranche amount is
bounded and complete, and `mul_div_floor` is exact. Schedule harnesses stub the
division with the expression it was proven equal to.

**Rationale.** Every branch and boundary of the logic is reachable inside the
bounded domain, so a logic error has a counterexample there. Overflow in the
256-bit intermediates, which only large values reach, is covered separately by
committed vectors from an arbitrary-precision oracle, regenerated in CI.
Mutating the end-of-schedule branch or the milestone check makes the
corresponding harness fail.

**Trade-offs.** The bounded domain is stated, not hidden; large-value behaviour
rests on the vectors rather than on the proofs.

**Where.** [`src/vesting/proofs.rs`](../src/vesting/proofs.rs); CI job `kani` in
[`.github/workflows/ci.yml`](../.github/workflows/ci.yml).

**Options considered.** Show refusals only in host tests; send refusals to the
